# Kombú admin recovery and execution plan

Baseline: 13 November 2026, revision `75cda48`. The brief uses “Com Modo”; the repository and application identify the product as Kombú.

The panel already has working business logic and fixture coverage for sales, reservations, dashboard finances, and storefront integration. English now covers every admin screen, dialog and message (15 November, branch `admin/recovery-phase2`). Purchase document capture, per-user permissions, and transaction-level recovery still need validation or implementation. Existing checks establish a local baseline. Release remains gated on staging reconciliation and operator workflows.

## Current system

`admin.html` loads `assets/admin.js`, which renders modules, applies business rules, stores local state, and syncs a shared JSON document via `api/state.js`. The API reconciles reservations, validates state and checks for regressions against the stored state. The client also uses dedicated sales, lead, audit, media and authentication routes. `supabase/schema.sql` defines shared state, backups, audit events, leads, email events and sales ledger. `supabase/storefront.sql` defines storefront orders and creator codes. Checkout, webhook and reservations code connect public orders to operational stock. Verify each write boundary in staging before changing the data model.

- [x] **P0** Value ingredients and packaging at weighted average cost (owner decision, 15 November 2026). Completed the same day.
  - The defect: registering a purchase added its quantity to stock and replaced `costPerUnit`/`costEach` with that purchase's price, silently revaluing stock bought earlier. Editing a purchase did the same. Deleting one removed stock but left the cost alone. Saving the ingredient form for any reason (a rename, a new minimum) recomputed the cost from the last package and overwrote it.
  - The fix: `receiveAtAverageCost` blends each purchase with the stock on hand. Stock with no known cost is valued at the incoming price instead of zero, and with nothing on hand the purchase price is used. `returnAtAverageCost` takes a purchase back out on edit or delete. The ingredient form now recomputes cost only when the package fields actually change. The purchase audit entry records the resulting average.
  - Evidence: `scripts/admin-logic-regression.js` covers receipt, reversal, empty stock and uncosted stock. The new browser suite `scripts/purchase-cost-regression.js` records, edits and deletes a purchase through the real forms, renames the ingredient and reloads, at 390 and 1440 px. Against the previous code it fails ("expected 0.03, got 0.035"). All node and six browser suites pass.
  - Production cost history is unaffected: batches store their cost when produced (`costSnapshot`), so later average changes do not restate past COGS. Batches created before snapshots existed still fall back to current recipe costs.
  - Limits:
    - Existing items keep their current unit cost (the last purchase price) as the opening average. History is not recomputed, because consumption since each purchase is not recorded per purchase.
    - When a past purchase is edited or deleted after part of that stock was consumed, the reversal is approximate. If the remaining value would fall to zero or below, the current average is kept rather than invented. Exact correction needs the movement ledger from the posting-contracts task.
- [x] **P0** Negative supply stock: allow with a warning (owner decision, 15 November 2026). Completed the same day.
  - Before: production already consumed ingredients and packaging below zero, with no warning and nothing flagged afterward. Purchase edits and deletions clamped stock at zero, hiding real deficits.
  - Now: new batches, batch quantity corrections, flavor stock adjustments and batch edits list every supply the production would take below zero, and ask before saving. Cancelling saves nothing and keeps the form open. Batch edits are judged on their net effect (`supplyShortfalls`, plus a new `beforeSave` hook on `editRecordForm`). Confirming keeps the true negative balance. "Atenção necessária" lists each negative supply in red until a purchase or count covers it. Reversals keep negatives instead of clamping. Stock fields no longer have a zero minimum, so a negative item can still be edited. The server already accepts negative material stock (`stateInvariantViolations` in `api/_lib/kombu-backend.js`).
  - Refinement for alert fatigue: only supplies whose stock is actually kept are warned about and flagged. That means items with a purchase on record, a minimum above zero, or a manual count (`stockCountedAt`, set when stock is entered by hand). Every recipe also consumes labels and date stamps that the seed data never counts, so warning on them would fire on every production. Untracked items still go negative silently, as before.
  - Quantities in the warning use the new `quantity()` formatter ("100 g", not "100,000 g", which reads as a hundred thousand in pt-BR).
  - Evidence: `scripts/admin-logic-regression.js` checks netting, tracked versus untracked items, the kept negative balance, the dashboard flag and its clearing. The new browser suite `scripts/supply-shortfall-regression.js` runs the real batch form at 390 and 1440 px: cancel saves nothing, confirm keeps -20 g, the red alert shows, a negative ingredient can be renamed, and covered stock produces no warning. Against the previous code it fails ("a warning is shown before saving"). The English text of the warning is checked in `npm run test:i18n`. All node and seven browser suites pass, and the alert was reviewed visually at 390 px.
- [x] **P0** Selling bottles beyond stock, the second half of the same decision. The owner approved the mechanism on 15 November 2026, and it was completed the same day.
  - How it works: after a warning, the excess is recorded as a sale with no batch (`recordPendingSale`, `pendingBatch: true`). Its revenue counts at once. Its cost stays unknown, so Reports withholds profit as it already did for uncosted sales. The server's over-allocation check ignores it.
  - Flavors with no stock: the quick sale and the sale form list every active product, including flavors with no stock ("Sem estoque · próximo lote"). The preview says the sale will wait for the next batch.
  - Splitting: a sale larger than its batch splits into a linked part and a pending part. Discount and delivery are charged once.
  - Still blocked:
    - The excess goes pending only when no other batch of that product still has bottles: physical bottles for the quick sale, which can take reserved bottles, and free bottles for the sale form. Otherwise the operator is told to choose the other batch.
    - Losses ("perda") can never exceed stock.
  - Linking: when a batch is created, corrected upwards, adjusted from Estoque or edited, `linkPendingSalesToBatch` absorbs pending sales oldest first, splitting where needed. This runs before order reservations, because those bottles have already left.
  - Display: the dashboard lists "Vendido sem estoque" per product in red, and the flavor row shows "N sem lote".
  - Evidence: `scripts/admin-logic-regression.js` covers revenue now and profit later, oldest-first linking with a split, the discount kept once, unchanged revenue, and the server's own `stateInvariantViolations` (no `batch_over_allocated`), `validateAppState` and `reconcileReservations` on a state with a pending sale. The new browser suite `scripts/oversell-regression.js` runs the real forms at 390 and 1440 px: cancel saves nothing, confirm records the pending sale, the dashboard flag shows and then clears when a batch is registered, a 5-bottle sale against 3 left splits 3+2 with one discount, the other-batch and loss blocks hold, and the state survives a reload. Against the previous code it fails. All 12 node suites and 9 browser suites pass, and the quick sale was reviewed at 390 px.
  - Limit: linking follows sale date, not the moment of sale within a day. The storefront's public availability counts only linked sales, so until the next batch is registered it does not show the deficit. With no batch there is nothing to offer anyway.
